# v0.7.0 — Elemental Enemies and Presentation

Status: In development

v0.7.0 adds elemental enemies, connected elemental presentation, shared
ballistic throws, movement-prediction parity, and a darker dungeon-atmosphere
pass. It extends the existing authoritative projectile, status, area,
rendering, and replication systems rather than creating parallel models.

## Shipped baseline — preserve, do not rebuild

- The authoritative `AreaSystem` already replicates fire, wet, oil, poison,
  smoke, and steam with deterministic duration and spread plus sanctuary- and
  height-aware placement. Validated `areaReactions.json` rules handle fire–oil
  and fire–wet reactions; vodka places oil, torches ignite it, and water flasks
  place wet areas.
- The client already renders pooled fire/poison particles, steam, and oil/wet
  surface treatments. Extend these systems; do not add a parallel ground-effect
  model or elemental renderer.
- Ballistic enemies already use authoritative three-dimensional
  `launchVelocity` and `stepProjectile` arcs. New arcing attacks reuse that path.
- Preserve the v0.6 dungeon baseline: district terrain with 32×32 runtime
  chunks, bedrock, optional void terrain, inside-room presentation, all room
  types, mini-boss containment, terrain-aware enemy sight and memory, elevation,
  and sanctuary rules.
- Preserve kid/touch assisted aim, mobile controls, unified revive feedback,
  and the responsive/customizable HUD.

## Implemented scope

### Pitchbloom

- Add **Pitchbloom** as a distinct enemy with its own stable content ID.
  Preserve Plant Creeper (`plant-creeper`), including its current behavior,
  animations, sprite mapping, and live global/biome population.
- Use existing authoritative targeting, line-of-sight, elevation, sanctuary,
  arena-containment, and ballistic-projectile rules to lob an oil ball toward a
  deterministic landing point.
- A direct hit applies the existing bounded `oiled` status. Any other impact
  creates exactly one deterministic 2×2 oil footprint at the authoritative
  impact; clients never infer hits, misses, or puddles.
- Keep range, cooldown, arc, speed, targeting error, duration, and damage as
  named content/configuration values.
- Restore the authored leafy Plant Creeper source art as crisp 24×24 atlas
  frames. Pitchbloom uses the same plant family silhouette with a distinct oily
  palette and a dedicated 16×16 oil-lob projectile.

### Chort flame

- Give **Chort** the directional flame attack. Preserve Greater Demon, Spitter's
  poison ranged attack and imp sprite, Wogol's beast identity, and the rest of
  the current roster.
- Use deterministic windup, active, recovery, and cooldown phases. The accepted
  flame reaches no farther than 3 world tiles.
- Resolve an ordered, height-aware segment on the server. Walls, bedrock, void
  and room boundaries, sanctuary, and arena containment block or suppress it;
  rendering cannot extend damage beyond the accepted segment.
- Apply bounded fire damage/status and ignite oil through the existing
  area-interaction path. Per-target throttling prevents duplicate burst damage.
- Replicate facing and attack phase for a shared connected flame and impact
  point, with bounded particles and reduced-effects support.

### Oiled entities and attribution

- Spatial effects use bounded composed cells with explicit `surface`, `flame`,
  and `gas` channels. Each channel keeps its own definition, duration, spread
  state, and source attribution; complete cells replicate atomically.
- Area reactions are declarative, deterministic data over known base effects.
  Closed-vocabulary actions may add, remove, transform, or continuously consume
  a matched layer. Schema validation rejects unknown references, unsafe numeric
  values, duplicate IDs, and unbounded/cyclic behavior.
- Fire contacting an entity with the v0.6 `oiled` status performs one
  idempotent transition to burning, consumes or replaces oil, and cannot
  refresh damage or duration indefinitely.
- Fire contact is bidirectional across the existing systems: a burning entity
  ignites oil beneath it, and a flying torch ignites an oiled entity it
  actually intersects. Neither interaction creates fire on bare ground.
- Ignited ground oil remains replicated and visible beneath the fire while its
  finite fuel timer burns down. Consuming the oil does not extinguish the
  already-created fire, which completes its own bounded lifetime.
- Preserve source attribution through placement and ignition for damage, kill
  credit, and combat messaging.
- Extend existing area/status schemas only as required for attribution and
  synchronized entity ignition. Preserve current fire–oil, fire–wet, spread,
  expiry, rendering, and throwable behavior.

### Movement modifier prediction parity

- Continuous movement modifiers—`slowed`, `wet`, the shipped `oiled`, and
  future modifiers—must use the same effective speed multiplier during
  authoritative movement, client prediction, and pending-step reconciliation
  for the status state and tick being replayed. Design the protocol and/or a
  shared resolver so this is generic rather than hardcoded to one debuff,
  preserves stacking parity, and handles modifier application and expiry
  without rubberbanding. Add focused multi-tick tests for applying, expiring,
  and stacked speed modifiers.

### Shared ballistic item throws

- Every parabolic player-thrown item uses one absolute target contract for
  preview, release, authoritative range clamping, landing elevation, and launch
  velocity. A short throw remains short; only targets beyond maximum range are
  clamped.
- Item-specific behavior begins at impact. Torches remain persistent,
  recoverable light entities, while bottles and future breakable items retain
  their authored impact and break behavior.
- Keep the old direction-only torch message as a compatibility adapter for
  older clients, but do not use it from the Phaser gameplay path.

### Elemental presentation

- Edge-sharing tiles of the same water, oil, or poison type render as one
  connected visual footprint. A lone tile reads as a rounded central puddle;
  larger shapes, including 2×2 and 4×4 areas, use neighbor-aware perimeter
  edges and remove internal seams without promising a perfect circle.
- Water may adapt the performant pooled blood-ground-shape mechanics into larger
  blue puddles, but uses its own semantic recipe and assets. Water gameplay and
  visibility never depend on blood or carnage settings.
- Oil and poison share the connected-footprint architecture but remain
  distinct: oil is dark and reflective; poison is hazardous and may animate
  within bounded budgets.
- Fire combines animated flame sprites with bounded pooled flame, ember, and
  spark particles plus light. Floor flames and every supporting particle read
  at the same deliberate scale; burning actors use a smaller, tightly attached
  version of the same flame-and-spark language.
- Each fire rig layers a small fixed number of phase-offset flame sprites so it
  flickers, leans, and breathes without per-frame allocation or an unbounded
  particle system.
- Edge-sharing fire tiles present as one connected burn field. Flame layers and
  emitters are distributed across the union rather than repeated as one centered
  badge per tile; they may cross internal fire edges but never spill onto
  non-burning cells.
- Poison pools use broad pooled bubbles distributed over their connected
  footprint, not point sparks. Poisoned actors use a strong partial-green tint
  plus an unmistakable bounded gas treatment that remains legible on green
  sprites and at normal gameplay zoom.
- Grouping is client presentation only; the authoritative `AreaSystem` remains
  tile-based. Use incremental dirty-region rebuilds, pooling, offscreen culling,
  and reduced-effects/mobile budgets—never per-frame flood-fill, allocation, or
  full-map rebuilds.
- Preserve world depth and occlusion. Connected visuals split and merge cleanly
  as replicated area tiles change. Ground liquids render above terrain but
  below actors and items; fire, steam, and airborne particles render above both.
- Poison applies the same configurable partial-green tint to players and
  enemies. Pooled fire maintains a persistent flame core and overlapping
  emissions so it cannot blink between particle cycles.

### Dungeon atmosphere

- Outside dungeon terrain has near-zero ambient illumination. The player,
  placed or flying torches, and authored wall torches provide the principal
  visibility; elemental light remains a bounded decorative accent.
- Use one low-resolution, refresh-capped darkness mask rather than a
  full-resolution shader or per-tile light GameObjects. Light reveal respects
  terrain occlusion and height where the current ground-light rules do.
- Add one or two slow, camera-following fog layers and one batched,
  fixed-capacity ambient-mote emitter. Constrained devices use lower mask
  resolution and particle budgets.
- Disable dungeon fog and motes in spawn, safe, party, and personal rooms.
  Interior presentation and room lighting remain readable and unchanged.
- Keep all ambient alpha, reveal radii, mask resolution, fog drift, and particle
  budgets in the shared lighting visual configuration.

## Guardrails

- Enemy decisions, collision, status changes, area placement, ignition, damage,
  and expiry remain server-authoritative and deterministic.
- New attacks must not bypass LOS memory, attacker limits, enemy jump physics,
  mini-boss containment, room visibility, spawn protection, or sanctuary.
- Hostile effects cannot leak across blocked terrain or interior boundaries, or
  render through walls and HUD layers.
- Active areas, spread work, projectiles, particles, lights, rebuilds, and
  network events remain bounded for mobile and slower machines.
- Multiplayer clients receive the same launch, impact, flame extent, status,
  ignition, and replicated footprint changes.

## Non-goals

- Rebuilding terrain, rooms, controls, HUD, revive UI, navigation, or the
  existing area/VFX foundation.
- A complete elemental crafting tree, every elemental interaction, or final
  balance for the full roster.
- Client-only combat outcomes or an unbounded fluid/fire simulation.
- Volumetric fog, a full-screen per-pixel lighting shader, or unbounded
  atmospheric particles.
