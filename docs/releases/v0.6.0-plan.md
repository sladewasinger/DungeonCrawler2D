# v0.6.0 — Elements Plan

Status: Planned

v0.6.0 is the first elemental-combat release. It moves enemy attacks and
ground effects onto explicit, data-driven behaviors, then makes the first
interaction—**oil + fire = ignition**—playable and observable.

This release must preserve server authority, deterministic simulation, and the
existing world/snapshot contracts. It is not a request to scatter special
cases through enemy AI or client rendering.

## Release goals

- Replace the current plant enemy with a generated 16×16 oil-monster plant
  named **Pitchbloom**. It lobs oil balls at players and creates oil patches
  when those throws miss.
- Rename the current `spitter` enemy to **Imp** and replace its ranged spit
  with a short-range fire burst/flamethrower attack.
- Give the Imp a visible fire sprite/animation that reaches a maximum of **3 m**
  in bursts rather than a poison projectile.
- Rework water, oil, fire, and other ground effects into a coherent simulation
  and rendering model with readable sprites, particles, duration, spread, and
  interaction rules.
- Make oil ignite when touched by the Imp's fire or another valid fire source.

## Issue 1 — Enemy role and naming migration

### Current behavior

The content roster currently has `plant-creeper` and `spitter`. The spitter
owns the delayed ranged-projectile AI, while the plant enemy is a close-range
attacker. The client and server also use these ids in tests, editor brushes,
population rosters, kill announcements, sprite resolution, and snapshots.

### Required content contract

- Replace the current plant-creeper content entry with the oil-monster plant
  **Pitchbloom**. Do not retain the old close-range plant behavior or the
  “Plant Creeper” player-facing name.
- Prefer canonical content ids `pitchbloom` and `imp`. If changing ids would
  invalidate saves, replay data, editor maps, or network messages, retain
  `plant-creeper` and `spitter` as read/migration aliases while
  emitting only the canonical ids for new content. Do not silently map an old
  id to the wrong enemy behavior.
- The former spitter becomes **Imp**. Its player-facing name, kill-line noun,
  editor label, debug label, and accessibility text must all say “Imp.”
- Sprite ids, animation prefixes, blood/gore tint, epithet text, population
  rosters, boss/content references, and test fixtures must follow the same
  canonical mapping. There must be one source of truth for the mapping.

### Tests

- New spawns and snapshots use the canonical ids and display names.
- Legacy ids load as the correct renamed enemy and never swap behaviors.
- Kill announcements, editor brushes, sprite lookup, and enemy showcase use
  the new names and animations.

## Issue 2 — Pitchbloom oil-lob attack

### Required behavior

- Pitchbloom uses a lobbed oil-ball attack, not a direct line projectile. It
  chooses a valid player target using the existing aggro rules, predicts a
  deterministic landing point, and launches an arcing oil ball toward it.
- The oil ball must have a visible airborne arc and impact point. A direct hit
  coats the player in an authoritative `oiled` status; it does not immediately
  create a ground puddle under the player unless the interaction table says the
  excess oil spills there.
- A miss resolves at the predicted/actual landing cell and creates oil across a
  bounded **2×2 tile area**. The server owns the landing result and effect
  creation; the client must not guess a miss or paint a local puddle.
- Range, cooldown, launch arc, projectile speed, targeting error, oil duration,
  status duration, and damage are content data/configuration—not literals hidden
  in the AI implementation.
- Reuse the server projectile/event path so remote clients see the same launch,
  flight, landing, direct-hit status, or 2×2 oil creation event.
- Oil-ball collision must respect walls, void walls, room boundaries, height,
  sanctuary suppression, dead/downed targets, and the existing projectile rules.

### Visual requirements

- Provide a readable oil-ball sprite with a visible launch, airborne arc, splash,
  and impact effect at the game's 3× display scale using nearest-neighbor
  sampling. The ball must be distinguishable from Imp fire and player weapons
  on desktop and mobile screens.

### Pitchbloom sprite/art brief

Generate a purpose-built **16×16 pixel-art oil monster plant**: a squat,
recognizable plant creature with a dark tar/oil palette, a readable opening or
pod that explains the lobbed oil balls, and one or two animation frames for
idle/walk/windup/release/recover. Use crisp hard-edged clusters and deliberate
silhouette contrast; do not upscale painted art or reuse the old plant sprite.
Keep the source art at 16×16 and let Phaser scale it with nearest-neighbor
filtering. Include separate 16×16 oil-ball and splash/impact sprites, with
transparent backgrounds and atlas-safe padding. The approved display name is
**Pitchbloom** unless art review selects a more fitting plant/oil name before
implementation; whichever name is approved must be canonical everywhere.

## Issue 3 — Imp burst flamethrower

### Required behavior

- Imp is a close-range fire attacker. Replace its poison projectile with a
  directional burst that extends up to **3 m** from the Imp toward its target.
- Model the attack as a short windup, one or more active burst ticks, and a
  recovery/cooldown. The active interval and tick rate must be named,
  data-driven tunables so balance can change without rewriting AI.
- Resolve the burst as a blocked ray/ordered segment test. Walls, void walls,
  room boundaries, height differences, and sanctuary rules must stop or suppress
  the effect according to the existing authoritative world rules.
- Apply fire damage/status to valid entities in the burst, with per-target hit
  throttling so one burst cannot deal unbounded damage every simulation tick.
- When the flame reaches an oil ground effect, emit the authoritative ignition
  event described in Issue 4. Fire should not require a projectile sprite to
  travel the full distance.
- Remote players must see the same facing, windup, active flame, impact, and
  recovery states. The client must not invent a longer or brighter flame than
  the server accepted.

### Visual requirements

- Use a real fire sprite/animation for the flame, not a poison blob, debug line,
  or tinted horizontal rule.
- Render the burst as connected flame segments or a tapered short cone, capped
  at 3 m, with animated flicker and a bright core/soft edge that reads as fire
  against dark floors.
- Add a small burst/ember particle effect at the origin and flame tip. Respect
  carnage/accessibility and reduced-motion settings.

### Tests and evidence

- Imp transitions through windup, active burst, and recovery deterministically.
- Burst length never exceeds 3 m and stops at blocking geometry.
- Damage/status is applied once per configured tick and cannot double-hit from
  client rendering.
- Sanctuary, height, dead/downed, and out-of-range targets follow the existing
  combat rules.
- Browser evidence shows a 3 m flame burst from the Imp at several facings,
  including a wall-blocked case and a remote-client view.

## Issue 4 — Ground-effect simulation and elemental interactions

### Required model

Replace ad hoc water/oil/fire handling with a shared `GroundEffect` model. Each
effect must have an authoritative type, owner/source, world position/area,
height layer, intensity, remaining duration, spread rules, and interaction
state. The server simulates effects; clients render snapshots/events and may
interpolate only visual progress.

Initial effect types:

- `water`: persistent wet area that can extinguish or weaken fire according to
  the interaction table.
- `oil`: flammable puddle that persists until consumed, expires, or is cleaned
  up; it should be visibly distinct from water.
- `fire`: burning area created by the Imp, ignited oil, torches, or future items;
  it deals configured tick damage, emits heat/fire status, and has a finite
  lifetime.
- Existing ground effects not yet receiving a new interaction should migrate
  to the model without changing behavior accidentally; document their current
  semantics and mark them for later element passes.

### Oil + fire interaction (first elemental chain)

- When a fire source contacts an oil area, the server emits one deterministic
  `oil-ignited` transition and converts the affected oil footprint to burning
  fire with the configured duration/intensity.
- A direct Pitchbloom hit applies an `oiled` status to the player for a bounded,
  visible duration. If the Imp's flame contacts an oiled player, emit one
  deterministic `oil-ignited-on-entity` transition, replace/augment the oil
  status with burning, and apply the configured fire damage over time. Repeated
  flame ticks must not restart the status indefinitely.
- Ignition must be idempotent: repeated flame ticks do not create duplicate
  effect entities, stack infinite damage, or reset duration unexpectedly unless
  the interaction table explicitly allows refresh.
- Burning oil may spread only through the configured neighboring cells/area,
  with bounded per-tick work and deterministic ordering.
- The interaction must respect sanctuary suppression, height/occlusion, water
  extinguishing rules, effect immunities, and cleanup/expiry.
- Broadcast the transition and resulting damage/status events to all relevant
  clients so remote players see the same ignition moment and footprint.

### Interaction table

Create a single data-driven table for effect interactions. At minimum document
water ↔ fire, oil ↔ fire, fire ↔ flammable entities, and duplicate effects of
the same type. Do not encode the matrix separately in enemy AI, item actions,
server ticks, and render code.

## Issue 5 — Ground-effect art and rendering overhaul

### Required visual pass

- Replace placeholder/debug ground-effect sprites with purpose-built pixel art
  for water, oil, fire, and their transition states.
- Fire must read as fire: animated flame shapes, bright core, ember/spark
  particles, flickering light/heat cue, and a clear burned/expired transition.
- Water must read as a wet pooled surface; oil must read as a darker reflective
  slick before ignition. Do not reuse the same tinted decal for all effects.
- Keep source art at native resolution and scale through Phaser's nearest-pixel
  sampling. Do not bake a separate 3× copy into large terrain textures.
- Ground effects must depth-sort with terrain, walls, stairs, entities, and
  void boundaries correctly. They must not draw through walls or appear above
  HUD/chat overlays.
- Particle counts, lifetimes, and pooled objects must be bounded and respect
  mobile/reduced-effects settings. A visual improvement must not recreate the
  texture-memory pressure targeted by v0.5.0.

### Rendering tests/evidence

- Snapshot/entity tests cover effect type, position, height, intensity, and
  expiry fields.
- Client tests cover sprite selection, nearest-neighbor scale, depth ordering,
  particle cleanup, and reduced-effects behavior.
- Capture before/after browser evidence for water, oil, fresh fire, burning oil,
  extinguished fire, and expired/cleaned ground effects at multiple elevations.

## Networking, performance, and compatibility gates

- Server remains authoritative for enemy decisions, projectile/flame hits,
  ground-effect creation, spread, ignition, damage, status, and expiry.
- Event/snapshot schemas are versioned or backward-compatible with clients that
  do not yet know the new effect type. Unknown visual effects must fail closed
  without breaking the rest of the frame.
- Bound active effect count, spread work, particle pools, and network event
  rates. Avoid one entity or burning puddle generating unbounded per-tick work.
- Deterministic seeded tests must produce identical enemy attacks and elemental
  transitions for the same world state.
- Existing sanctuary, party, height, loot, accessibility, carnage, and remote
  player visibility rules remain intact.

## Non-goals

- A complete elemental crafting tree or every future element.
- Final balance for all damage, cooldown, spread, and duration values beyond
  the explicit 3 m Imp flame and initial oil/fire interaction.
- Replacing the entire enemy roster or redesigning unrelated enemy sprites.
- Client-only elemental effects that disagree with the server.

## Definition of done

- The former plant creeper is replaced by a clearly named Pitchbloom oil
  monster, and the former spitter is consistently named Imp across content, UI,
  tools, tests, kill lines, and network-compatible migrations.
- Pitchbloom's oil-ball lobs and Imp's 3 m burst flame work in solo and
  multiplayer, with authoritative targeting, status, landing, and blocking.
- A direct oil-ball hit applies the authoritative `oiled` status; a miss creates
  exactly one bounded 2×2 oil area at the authoritative landing point.
- The Imp flame uses fire art, animation, particles, and bounded pooled visual
  effects; no debug line or poison projectile remains on its attack path.
- Water, oil, and fire use the shared ground-effect model and readable sprites.
- Oil + fire reliably produces one synchronized, visible ignition transition,
  with deterministic spread, damage/status, expiry, and extinguishing behavior.
- Ground effects depth-sort correctly, remain visible to remote players, and
  honor sanctuary, accessibility, reduced-motion, and mobile performance rules.
- Focused engine, server integration, client rendering, schema, and migration
  tests pass, with browser evidence attached to the release review.
